FMCSA CDL Training Providers Dashboard

This dashboard tracks the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration's (FMCSA) review and removal of accredited Commercial Driver's License (CDL) training providers from its certification registry.

Background

The FMCSA began removing thousands of CDL training providers from its certification registry in the fall of 2025. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy argued the purge was necessary to reign in "illegal and reckless practices that let poorly trained drivers get behind the wheel of semi-trucks and school buses," referring to a decades-long pattern of fraudulent licensing practices in the commercial trucking industry. The move came on the heels of the Department of Transportation's introduction of new rules limiting which classes of immigrants are eligible for CDLs, though a federal appeals court paused the implementation of those rules last November.

Many of the removed providers were small businesses and municipal governments that occasionally trained employees to operate commercial vehicles. Many others had not provided training services in years, if ever. Critics of the FMCSA's actions have argued that the agency's review process has been haphazard and unfair, with many providers being removed without clear explanation or opportunity for appeal.

While California, Texas and Illinois saw the largest number of removals in absolute terms, when adjusted for the number of active providers in each state, states like Kansas, Washington and Wisconsin saw higher removal rates relative to the total number of accredited CDL training providers in those states.
